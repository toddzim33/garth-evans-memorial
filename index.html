About Garth

He made people feel like the interesting thing was just about to begin.

Garth grew up in Prince George, found his way through film and video production, and eventually discovered that technology in service of education and people was where he belonged.

At UBC Okanagan, he became a trusted guide through complicated systems and even more complicated possibilities. He listened, learned, helped, and connected people who might otherwise never have met.

“It’s not just the technology. It’s what people can do when the technology gets out of their way.”

A working sentiment drawn from the pattern of Garth’s writing and work—not a direct quotation.

A career, becoming

He started with a job description.
Then he kept looking outward.

This isn’t a résumé. It’s the shape of a person becoming more fully himself at work.

01

2012 · The job was support

Classrooms, cameras, cables, calls.

Garth arrived at UBC as an Audio Visual Support Analyst. The role was practical and bounded: support classrooms, maintain systems, troubleshoot problems, train users, and keep videoconferencing working across a distributed medical program.

The original position description already asked for collaboration, analytical thinking, empathy, and understanding the relationships between problems. Garth would spend the next decade taking those words seriously.

02

2015–2016 · The work gets bigger

He kept finding the larger system inside the immediate problem.

He was still supporting the Southern Medical Program, but he was also helping shape collaboration services, working with Compute Canada and WestGrid, contributing to procurement and service design, and helping other teams understand what was possible.

“I’d like to spend more time learning about what’s really happening in all corners of the UBCO and UBC campuses and maybe even volunteer on a committee or two. I need to become more engaged with the institution as a whole.”

— Garth, 2016 self-assessment
03

2018–2019 · Get out more

Curiosity became a professional method.

Garth wanted to see what other universities were doing, meet people outside his immediate area, understand research across the institution, and learn where digital media and higher education were heading. He attended conferences, visited other campuses, and brought those conversations home.

“I’m a great listener; although not a technical proficiency, I find that this skill is serving me in increasing capacities in relationship building.”

— Garth, 2019 self-assessment

“Garth has become the ‘go-to’ expert on campus.”

— Todd, 2018 review
04

2020–2023 · Beyond the job description

Technical support became consultation, then community.

During the pandemic, Garth supported remote teaching, research grants, live events, audio standards, onboarding, and a volunteer mental-health podcast. At the same time, he helped bring VEMS from an ambitious project into a working environment, and helped shape Constellation into something people could gather around.

The title changed later. The direction had been visible for years.

05

2024 · The community self-manifests

He became the person people called when they didn’t yet know who to call.

By the end, Garth’s work crossed research, teaching, emerging media, visualization, procurement, public engagement, and institutional imagination. He had spent years making connections until the connections began making themselves.

“The opportunities to connect people otherwise unconnected is starting to present itself more and more, the community building is truly starting to self-manifest which is rewarding.”

— Garth, 2024 self-assessment

He became interested.
He learned.
He found people.
He helped.
He connected them.
He built something.
Then he noticed something else interesting.
